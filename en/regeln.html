Rules

If you know normal chess, you can start after five minutes. Here are all the rules in full – from the board and the moves through the centre to the duty to help, which is what makes the three-player game fair.

Try it yourself

Tap a picture: the practice board opens with exactly this position. There you can also place any piece on any square and immediately see where it can move.

Players and board

  • Three players: Blue, Green, Red. Players move in turn: Blue → Green → Red.
  • The board is a Star of David made of three sections of 4 × 8 squares each: rows A–D, columns 1–8. Row A is your own back rank, row D lies at the centre of the board.
  • Each player has 16 pieces as in normal chess. The king stands on A5, the queen on A4.

Moves across the centre

  • Whoever moves beyond their own row D enters a neighbouring section – on its row D – and continues there towards its row A.
  • The column is mirrored: column c becomes column 9 − c.
  • Rook and queen (straight): From columns 1–3 you reach one neighbouring section, from 6–8 the other. From the middle columns 4 and 5 both sections can be reached.
  • Bishop and queen (diagonal): A bishop also crosses into a neighbouring section, at the tip of the centre into both.
  • Knight: two steps forward/back and one sideways (or the other way round), also across the centre. A few transitions at the borders are excluded.
  • King: one square in every direction, also across into the neighbouring sections.
  • In the game you can try out every piece in the start window under “🎯 Practice board”.

Pawns

  • In their own section pawns move towards the centre (A → D), from row B also two squares.
  • Across the centre they reach a neighbouring section and continue there towards its row A. They capture diagonally forward, also across the centre.
  • Promotion: on row A of another player’s section.
  • En passant: After a double step both opponents may capture en passant until the player who made the double step moves again.

Castling

  • King (A5) and rook unmoved, squares in between empty and not attacked. Short: king to A7, rook A8 → A6. Long: king to A3, rook A1 → A4.

Check, duty to help and mate

  • Whoever is in check must get out of it. No move may leave your own king in check.
  • Duty to help: If a player is in check and the third player moves before him, that player may not make a move after which the threatened player would be mated. If the threatened player is already mated, the third player must free him – if he cannot, the mate stands. If the threatened player can save himself anyway, whatever the third player plays, there is no restriction.
  • Checkmate ends the game. The winner is whoever gives check to the king – not whoever only takes away escape squares. The other two lose.

No move possible

  • Whoever cannot move without being in check skips a turn. If nobody can move any more, the score decides.

Time and points

  • Optionally time per move (expired → the move is forfeited) and/or per game (used up → no more moves, the pieces can still be captured).
  • Points: own pieces on the board + captured enemy pieces. Pawn 1, knight and bishop 3, rook 4, queen 6. In case of a tie, whoever used less time wins.

Controls

  • Click a piece → possible moves light up → click the target square. “↶ Back” or Ctrl+Z takes moves back.
  • At the top of the game: “＋ New game”, “📁 Game” (save, load, move list) and “👁 View” (coordinates, rotate board, colours, full screen). Plus the 3D view, 🔊 sound, ⏸ pause and 👥 player windows.
  • The computer opponents are 8 AI opponents, each with its own look, voice and playing style: Blubbo, Grumbo, Flammo, Zappo, Spuki, Kraki, Sonno, Fluffi. In the start window you choose which computer opponent plays each computer seat. They rejoice, get angry, negotiate and form alliances – and with “🎤” you can simply talk to them.

The notation

Every square has a name of three characters: section (B, G or R for Blue, Green, Red), row (A to D) and column (1 to 8). So BA5 is square A5 in the blue section – where the blue king stands at the start. A move is written as start and target square, for example BB4–BD4 for a pawn’s double step; a capture with “×”, such as GC3×RD6.

Tip: In the game you can show the coordinates under “👁 View”. The move list at the top right shows every move in this notation, and with 📼 you can replay any game move by move.
