Chess Without Borders

Perfectly normal chess – only the board has no edge. Pieces may dodge to the left, right, forward and back as far as they like. With two players as usual, with three or four the armies sit as at a table.

▶ Play Chess Without Borders

The setup

  • With two players Blue and Green stand as in normal chess.
  • With three and four players the armies stand as at a table: Blue at the bottom, Red on the left, Green at the top, Yellow on the right. Between the armies lies an empty space of four rows. Each army has a coloured start zone of two rows.
  • Every pawn moves towards the player opposite.

The pieces

All pieces move as in normal chess. On the infinite board, however, rook, bishop and queen get very far: they may move up to ten squares beyond the outermost pieces – the game shows every reachable square.

Castling and en passant

  • Castling: King and rook must not have moved yet, the squares between them are empty, the king is not in check and does not pass over an attacked square. The king moves two squares towards the rook, the rook jumps over it.
  • En passant: If a pawn moves two squares, an enemy pawn may capture it in passing – with several players until the player who made the double step is to move again.

Promotion

  • With two players: as usual on the opponent’s back rank.
  • With three or four players the host decides: The pawn becomes a queen as soon as it enters another player’s start zone – or only when it reaches the admirals’ back rank of another player. Opposite, the extended back rank always counts too.

Check, mate and end of the game

  • Whoever is in check must get out of it. No move may leave your own king in check. A king in check glows red.
  • End of the game – chosen by the host: “First mate decides”: whoever gives mate wins. “Elimination”: whoever is mated is out and his pieces disappear; play goes on until one is left.
  • Whoever has only the king left is beaten. Whoever cannot move without being in check skips a turn.
  • Once a capture has happened: after 30 rounds without a capture and without a pawn move, material decides (pawn 1, knight and bishop 3, rook 5, queen 9).

Keeping the overview

  • Pan: drag. Zoom: mouse wheel or two fingers. Double-click: fit all pieces.
  • The overview at the bottom right shows everything that is happening; tap it to jump there.
  • Rotate board: own side at the bottom, towards the player to move or by hand.
  • Tap a piece: its moves appear green (free), red (capture) and blue (castling). Enemy pieces show their moves in light blue and orange, for viewing only. The last move is marked in the mover’s colour.

What all variants have in common

  • Opponents: Every seat can be a human on this device, a human invited online or a computer – in four strengths: easy, medium, strong and master. The master also thinks ahead about the opponents’ replies.
  • Computer opponents with character: The computer opponents are called Blubbo, Grumbo, Flammo, Zappo, Spuki, Kraki, Sonno, Fluffi. They rejoice and get angry on the monitors, forge alliances against the leader, sometimes betray them, take revenge – and speak if you like. You can negotiate with them in the chat (“Grumbo, let’s attack Red together!”). They also remember how the humans play. For each computer seat you choose which computer opponent plays – each has its own look, its own voice and its own playing style: Flammo charges ahead, Grumbo builds walls, Kraki builds up patiently, Fluffi greedily collects material, Spuki hunts the weakest. With “🎤” you can simply talk to them.
  • Duty to help (option from three players): If a player is in check, everyone who moves before him must help him: if he is mated, the move must free him; if he is not mated, the move must not checkmate him. Whoever cannot help seals the mate.
  • Clock: time per move, time per game or no clock. If the move time runs out, the move is forfeited; if the game time is used up, you are out.
  • Online: live with monitors, camera, microphone, chat, emoji reactions, subtitles and automatic translation – or as a correspondence game in which everyone moves when they have time (with a notification when it is your turn). Games can be offered in the public lobby; the host decides whether others may watch.
  • After the game: replay move by move, analysis board with suggestions from the computer, evaluation with key moments, psychological evaluation (revenge, nervousness after losses, silent alliances, said and done) and, on request, a professional AI report.
  • Also: freely selectable colours (also with signs for the colour-blind), sounds, pause, save and load, “My games”, introduction for newcomers, installable as an app, full screen.

Chess Without Borders is a draft and is being developed further.
