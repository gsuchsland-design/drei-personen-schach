Chess of the 6 Realms

A cube of 8 × 8 × 8 cells, with a realm on each of its six sides: a player’s home block. Up to six armies move into the cube from all sides – top, bottom, left, right, front, back. In short: Sd6R.

The playing field

  • The cube in the middle has 8 × 8 × 8 = 512 cells. It is empty at the start – this is where the fighting happens.
  • On each side lies a home block of 8 × 8 × 4 cells. That is where a player’s pieces stand at the start.
  • Together there are 2,048 cells. The corners and edges outside the cube are not part of the playing field.
  • Cells are named with three numbers from 1 to 16, for example 8·8·8 – the game shows the name of every cell you tap.

Players and order

The game is played by two, three, four or six players; free seats are taken by the computer if you wish. With two players Blue and Green face each other, with four Blue, Red, Green and Yellow sit in a circle around the cube, with six Purple (bottom) and Turquoise (top) join in. Players move in turn. Whoever invites decides how many play, who is a computer and how the game ends.

The setup

The standard is the large setup: on the outermost layer of the home block stand 64 admirals – eight rows, in the middle one the king with queen, rooks, bishops and knights, around them the special pieces. In front of them 64 pawns fill a whole layer. Two layers stay empty up to the cube. If you prefer it smaller, choose in the game one row of 8 pawns, two rows or a block of 4 × 4 pawns.

Directions in space

In the cube there are three kinds of directions from every cell:

  • Axes (6): one step left/right, forward/back or up/down.
  • Face diagonals (12): two axes at once – like the diagonal on a chessboard.
  • Space diagonals (8): all three axes at once – straight through a small cube from corner to corner.

The pictures show each piece in the middle of the empty cube; the green dots are the cells it can reach. Top left you see the piece as a spaceship; fairy pieces carry golden tips or extra wings. In the game you choose under “Pieces” between symbols, chess pieces, star pieces and spaceships.

The pieces

Rook

Straight along the three axes: left/right, forward/back, up/down – 6 directions. From the centre of the cube up to 45 cells.

Bishop

Along the face diagonals – two axes at once, like the bishop on a board, only in all three planes: 12 directions. As a setting also along the space diagonals (20 directions).

Unicorn

Only along the space diagonals – all three axes at once, from one corner of the cube to the opposite one: 8 directions. The piece comes from Ferdinand Maack’s Raumschach (1907).

Queen

Rook, bishop and unicorn in one piece: all 26 directions (as a setting without space diagonals: 18). From the centre up to 118 cells – the strongest classic piece.

King

One cell in each of the 26 directions (as a setting without space diagonals: 18). Every player has exactly one king.

Knight

Two cells along one axis and one along a second – 24 target cells, jumping over everything. As a setting additionally “2 + 1 + 1” across three axes (48 targets).

Nightrider

A knight’s jump that may be repeated in the same direction as often as you like, as long as every landing cell is free. In the empty cube it gets very far – the game shows the cells landed on along the way as dots.

Duchess

Rook + unicorn: axes and space diagonals.

Governor

Bishop + unicorn: face and space diagonals.

Archbishop

Bishop + knight.

Chancellor

Rook + knight.

Amazon

Queen + knight – the strongest piece in the game, up to 142 targets from the centre of the cube.

The pawns

  • Pawns move one cell straight ahead – always towards the opposite realm.
  • In their own home block they may move up to two cells every time (adjustable: only on the first move or always).
  • They capture diagonally forward: forward-left, forward-right, forward-up, forward-down – 4 directions (adjustable: also along the space diagonals, 8).
  • Promotion: When a pawn reaches the opposite home block, it becomes a queen (adjustable: only on its rearmost layer).

Check, mate and end of the game

  • Whoever is in check must get out of it. No move may leave your own king in check.
  • Whoever cannot move without being in check skips a turn.
  • End of the game – chosen by the host:
    “First mate decides”: as soon as a player is mated, the game ends; whoever gave check wins.
    “Elimination”: whoever is mated is out and his pieces disappear; play goes on until one is left.
  • Whoever has only the king left is beaten.
  • Once a capture has happened: after 30 rounds without a capture and without a pawn move, material decides (pawn 1, knight, bishop and unicorn 3, rook and nightrider 5, governor 5, archbishop 6, duchess and chancellor 7, queen 9, amazon 11).

Keeping the overview

  • Tap a piece: its moves light up green, captures red. Enemy pieces can be tapped too – their moves appear light blue and orange, for viewing only.
  • The last move is marked in the mover’s colour, with a line and all intermediate landings.
  • “🎯 My army” shows your own army, “Colours” fades or hides single realms, “Layer” picks out a single slice.
  • Rotate: drag. Zoom: mouse wheel or two fingers. Pan: right-click or two fingers.

What all variants have in common

  • Opponents: Every seat can be a human on this device, a human invited online or a computer – in four strengths: easy, medium, strong and master. The master also thinks ahead about the opponents’ replies.
  • Computer opponents with character: The computer opponents are called Blubbo, Grumbo, Flammo, Zappo, Spuki, Kraki, Sonno, Fluffi. They rejoice and get angry on the monitors, forge alliances against the leader, sometimes betray them, take revenge – and speak if you like. You can negotiate with them in the chat (“Grumbo, let’s attack Red together!”). They also remember how the humans play. For each computer seat you choose which computer opponent plays – each has its own look, its own voice and its own playing style: Flammo charges ahead, Grumbo builds walls, Kraki builds up patiently, Fluffi greedily collects material, Spuki hunts the weakest. With “🎤” you can simply talk to them.
  • Duty to help (option from three players): If a player is in check, everyone who moves before him must help him: if he is mated, the move must free him; if he is not mated, the move must not checkmate him. Whoever cannot help seals the mate.
  • Clock: time per move, time per game or no clock. If the move time runs out, the move is forfeited; if the game time is used up, you are out.
  • Online: live with monitors, camera, microphone, chat, emoji reactions, subtitles and automatic translation – or as a correspondence game in which everyone moves when they have time (with a notification when it is your turn). Games can be offered in the public lobby; the host decides whether others may watch.
  • After the game: replay move by move, analysis board with suggestions from the computer, evaluation with key moments, psychological evaluation (revenge, nervousness after losses, silent alliances, said and done) and, on request, a professional AI report.
  • Also: freely selectable colours (also with signs for the colour-blind), sounds, pause, save and load, “My games”, introduction for newcomers, installable as an app, full screen.

Chess of the 6 Realms is a draft and is being developed further. The move rules can be changed in the game under “Move rules to try out”.
