Chess in the stella octangula, the three-dimensional Star of David, for 2 to 8 players
Whoever reaches one of the 8 centre cells on a space diagonal may continue through the centre point into any other space diagonal – all the way into any point. Applies to unicorn, queen and king.

Fairy pieces that are deselected are replaced by classic ones in the setup: Unicorn → Bishop.
The stella octangula is the three-dimensional Star of David with 8 points. The board is an 8 × 8 × 8 cube folded into a star: 512 cells with 6 faces each, every corner of the cube is a point. Each army stands in one point. Tap a piece → its moves light up (green = free, red = capture). Drag = rotate, wheel/two fingers = zoom, right-click drag/two fingers = pan.
Tap a colour: ● visible → ◐ faded → ○ hidden. Pan: right-click/Shift + drag or with two fingers. Double-click = whole star.
Fairy pieces:
Rook ♜: straight through the faces of the cells. Bishop ♝: diagonally across the edges. Unicorn 🦄: across the corners. Wormhole (if the host switches it on): whoever reaches one of the 8 centre cells on a space diagonal can continue through the centre point into any other space diagonal. Queen ♛: all of these. King ♚: one step like the queen. Knight ♞: 2 + 1 across. Pawn ♟: one cell further away from its own point, captures diagonally forward; in the three outermost layers of a foreign point it becomes a queen. The placing tool also offers the fairy pieces from Chess of the 6 Realms.
With two players the armies stand at the poles, with three or four at the points of a tetrahedron, with six in a ring around the centre, with eight in all points. All 8 points are equivalent. Five or seven players also work, but it is not quite balanced (⚠️). Whoever cannot move without being in check skips a turn. Whoever has only the king left is beaten. Once the first capture has happened: after 30 rounds without a capture or a pawn move, material decides.
To place pieces, choose an area – then its cells can be clicked.
